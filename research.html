RESEARCH OVERVIEW / EXPERIMENTAL FRAMEWORK

Evaluating the resilience of business analytics

This page documents the experiment design and evaluation approach. The live Streamlit dashboard is the interactive companion for exploring the generated results.

01 / EVALUATION DIMENSIONS

What the framework measures

Each corrupted dataset is passed through the same evaluation pipeline and compared with the clean baseline.

01

Business KPIs

Total revenue, order count, units sold, average order value and revenue per customer.

02

Category analysis

Top category and category share, used to inspect whether categorical corruption changes business interpretation.

03

Segmentation stability

Customer segment changes compared with the baseline segmentation for customers present in both datasets.

04

Statistical analysis

ANOVA statistics and p-values are monitored descriptively across the repeated experiments.

05

Data completeness

Missing-cell counts and percentages show the extent of missingness in the evaluated fields.

06

Repeatability

Multiple random seeds per scenario help expose variation between independently generated corruptions.

Interpreting the noise percentage

The meaning of a noise percentage depends on the injector implementation. In the current design, the selected fraction is applied to eligible values/cells according to the chosen mechanism. The exact denominator and affected columns should be reported from the final injector code and not described as a percentage of all dataset cells unless that is how the code calculates it.
03 / INTERPRETATION & LIMITATIONS

What the results can — and cannot — establish

Dataset-specific findings

Observed sensitivity depends on the dataset, selected columns, corruption mechanism and evaluation pipeline. Results should not be generalized as universal thresholds.

Missingness assumptions

Randomly injected missing values model one controlled mechanism. They do not represent every real-world missing-data process.

Statistical claims

Mean ANOVA p-values alone do not establish false-positive or false-negative rates. Those require known ground truth and an explicit repeated testing design.
